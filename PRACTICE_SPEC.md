# Mech Royale practice policy for tigerbyte

This describes the shipped giddygiddyboy policy at `70c8b23fc1d829111897762d3da3ba28c34a14cf` and the Kanyaraasi comparison at `24d68e50ecd1ecb272b614b8a38c509bc8eeee5f`. Tigerbyte driver 2 uses the base policy.

Distances are horizontal unless specified, in metres. Times are match seconds from `t`, angles are radians, and yaw differences use the shortest signed turn in [−π, π]. Our HP fraction uses `hp` divided by the greater of 1 and `max_hp`. Use live `self.stats` and each equipped weapon’s `stats` for engine values. If `self.stats.radius` is 0, use the entire `config.mech` stat block for our mech.

## 1. Memory and observation updates

- Keep separate tables keyed by `id` for enemy mechs and drones. Replace the observed position, velocity, yaw and pitch, HP, max HP, cubes, held weapon kinds and charging flag each time an entity appears in `mechs`. Record that observation’s time and retain the previous last-hit-us timestamp. An entry is fresh when its recorded observation time equals current `t`, even if its ID is missing from a repeated-timestamp observation. Literal `npc` entries go in the drone table and all other kinds go in the mech table. The kit’s observation memory can supply an entity behind cover for 5 s.
- On a fresh entry, store XY distance and test a clear shot from our current muzzle to its midpoint at 50% of its height. For literal `mech` only, retry at 85% if the midpoint is blocked. Use configured enemy mech radius, height and eye height multiplied by 1 plus `config.cubes.size_per_cube` times its full cube count, with no size cap. Drones use configured drone radius and height. Omitted numeric observation fields decode as 0.
- When distance is >1, closing speed is the negative dot product of enemy-minus-own horizontal velocity with the unit vector from us to the enemy. At ≤1 keep the previous value, initially 0. Between observations, keep the last reported coordinates, velocity, HP and distance without extrapolating or subtracting damage from `hit` events. Prune mechs at age >20 s and drones at age >120 s.
- For fight awareness, include mech entries aged ≤2.5 s. Clear their engagement flags before reevaluating them. For entries with a recorded time different from current `t`, also clear their shot visibility and skip target selection. Their stored position can still count for cover and other-enemy sightlines. Older mech entries can contribute to the opening’s home search until age 20 s.
- Each `damaged` event with a non-null `by` updates the last incoming-hit time and the attacker’s last-hit-us time if its entry already exists. Update the incoming bearing only if supplied. Separately record the time and bearing of any damage whose weapon is NPC and whose bearing is supplied. Process events in input order. New attackers’ recorded times, the global incoming-hit time and the last-threat time start at −100 s. Bearings start at 0. A null attacker leaves the general damage clock unchanged. Each `kill` increments our kill count and removes that mech’s entry.
- Remember crate, cube and pickup IDs, kinds, positions, HP when supplied, and observation times. For an entry whose recorded time differs from current `t`, remove it only when age >0.35 s, distance <40 and there is a clear ray from our eye to 1.5 m above its last position. Otherwise retain it without a general expiry. Loot failure has a separate temporary blacklist (§7). Projectile trajectories use the current observation. Sounds, beam reports, hit amounts, status-effect reports and pickup result events do not drive decisions.
- Keep the zone’s hold circle, the last selected shot target and selection time, the last valid commanded aim, current loot goal and start time, sampled home point, route and waypoint index, cover goal, retreat and stuck clocks, strafe side and its deadline, and any planned escape launch and landing. Each is used and refreshed as described below.

## 2. Opening and squad phases

- Use the first processed observation’s `alive` as the baseline. After handling kills each observation, compute baseline alive minus current `alive` minus our counted kills. If the result is positive, increment a consecutive-observation counter by 1. Otherwise reset it to 0. At 5 consecutive positive observations, permanently disqualify the match from squad mode. The counter counts observations with an unexplained deficit, including repeated observations of the same deficit.
- Use the opening profile through and including exactly 20 s. Enter squad on the first processed observation with `t` >20 s if the disqualification has not latched. If it latches later, revert to the opening profile for the rest of the match. The switch replaces policy settings while retaining tracking, clocks, target history and navigation state. There is no mode bit from the server.
- The separate opening-loot preset in the source is disabled at this revision. The active opening already ranks laser first, has a 90 m pickup search radius, and scores two rings of fight steps. Squad changes targeting, loot and combat preferences as below and scores one ring. Both profiles enable drone farming and dodging. Random exploration, alternate economy rules, odds-based engagement and withdrawal, and optional supply-drop pursuit are disabled.

## 3. Target choice

1. Define an enemy as aimed at us when its current yaw is <0.22 from the bearing from its feet to ours. Define late match as next-zone radius ≤70 or `alive` ≤3.
2. Opening engagement accepts a fresh mech at distance <60, after it hit us <4 s ago, when it is aimed at us and charging at distance <120, when it has <350 raw HP at distance <120, or when `alive` ≤2. The distance cap is 215 for shot targeting.
3. Squad engagement accepts a fresh mech at distance <60, after it hit us <6 s ago, in late match, when it is closing at >3 m/s at distance <180, when its HP is <40% of its own max at distance <160, or when it is aimed at us at distance <190. The distance cap is 240 for shot targeting.
4. From engaged mechs with a clear shot and distance ≤ the phase’s cap, minimize distance plus HP weight times raw HP. Opening HP weight is 60/1400. Squad HP weight is 0.2. In squad subtract 100 at HP ≤30% of its max, then 120 for the last selected shot target if it was selected <2 s ago. In both phases then subtract 40 if it hit us <4 s ago, followed by 15 if closing speed is >3. Initialize the sticky target ID and selection time to 0. Every selection refreshes that 2 s clock, including opening selections. A missing target leaves the stored choice and time intact. Eligibility and the cap always come first. Neither raw HP nor the total score is clamped.
5. The nearest fresh engaged mech with a blocked shot and distance strictly below the cap is the fallback. If there is a shot target, prioritize it. If there is none, the opening always accepts the obscured fallback for movement and precharge. Squad accepts that one fallback only if its HP is <101% of max HP. There is no search for a second obscured candidate if that check fails. An obscured fallback never serves as a direct mech firing target.
6. Exact ties keep the first encountered in the Go map iteration for both shot score and obscured distance. There is no ID tie-break or draw from the seeded generator.

For movement and retreat, danger means any awareness entry is engaged or hit us <4 s ago. The crowd count includes each fresh mech that is engaged or has a clear shot at distance <150 by our shot test. This can count engaged entries beyond the target cap. Refresh the last-threat timestamp on danger or while the last incoming hit is <3 s old.

## 4. Weapons, detours and firing

### What to carry and when to detour

- Keep the spawn gun in slot 0. Consider a floor gun only if slot 0 exists and is empty. Reject a duplicate secondary and all mortar pickups. The secondary preference is laser 4, lightning 3, mortar 1 before rejection, and 0 for empty or unknown. A secondary candidate must strictly outrank the current slot 1. Search the full held-weapon array for duplicates and equipped weapons. The underlying laser rank is 2 when laser preference is disabled, a setting inactive in this snapshot’s shipped profiles.
- Seek wanted pickups only in the ordinary loot branch (§5), through the shared travel safety gates and loot score (§7). In opening their radius is strictly <90. In squad it is <150, except laser <240. Pickup value is 60, or 130 if the observed slot array has a slot 1 and it is null. In squad a wanted laser has value 150 regardless. There is no additional danger, own-HP or time-since-damage gate for this loot category.
- Independently of the movement branch, scan current `pickups` in observation order and take the first wanted item at distance ≤ `config.loot.pickup_radius` minus 0.3 and vertical separation ≤3.6. The protocol’s 4 m horizontal default gives 3.7 m. Use `take` alone when a slot is free. When both slots are occupied, set `drop` to 1 in the same action. Cancel the take and stop scanning if the take would swap two occupied slots and slot 1 holds a laser with `charge` >0. Never drop a weapon by itself.

### Preferred distances

| Movement preference, before finishing | Opening | Squad |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Gun or default, including a mortar-only loadout | 85 m | 85 m |
| Laser, if held and the lightning rule below did not win | 85 m | 120 m |
| Lightning, if held and our energy is >12 | 28 m | 28 m |
| Any weapon against a drone | 58 m | 58 m |

Against mechs, without lightning equipped, apply a floor of 64 m if the foe holds lightning and 80 m if it holds mortar. Apply both floors if needed. Having lightning equipped removes both floors even when its energy is ≤12. The squad finish rule temporarily replaces the three mech range preferences before floors (§6).

### Shared aim and lead

- Fire control runs after movement selection. Priority is the shot-target mech, then a drone, then a crate. For a mech, aim at its midpoint, or at 85% height if the midpoint ray from the predicted muzzle is blocked. Use that fallback without a second check at 85%. Drone aim is at its midpoint, crate aim at half configured crate height with zero velocity. The predicted muzzle shifts our XY by horizontal velocity times 0.075 s and uses current feet Z plus live muzzle height. Own vertical speed is unused.
- Gun lead begins with flight time 0 and performs 4 passes. On each, predict target XY at 0.075 s plus the current flight time. If absolute target vertical velocity is ≥0.5 m/s, also predict Z with half its vertical velocity over that same interval. Otherwise preserve target Z. Recompute flight time as the positive part of 3D muzzle-to-prediction distance minus 3 m, divided by weapon speed. Use 252 m/s if speed is ≤0. Aim at the fourth prediction.
- Instant weapon aim predicts target XY 0.125 s ahead and keeps its Z fixed. Compute each aim as the bearing and elevation from the predicted muzzle. Default to gun lead whenever a gun is fitted, including when it cannot shoot, and otherwise use instant aim.
- Use two-argument arctangent with 0.75 times target radius for yaw or 0.4 times target height for pitch, and the greater of 1 m and target distance as the second argument. Recompute the horizontal firing distance from our current feet to the target’s recorded position, including for remembered drones. Gun and mortar slew allowance each deduct 0.05 s times actual turn rate or pitch rate from the respective absolute angular error, flooring each at 0. Use actual `self.aim` for all firing tests. Clamp outgoing pitch to live min and max after choosing the aim.

### Trigger and reload decisions

Start with both triggers off. Visit slots 0 and 1 in that order. Each weapon tests alignment against the same observed torso aim. Later aim overrides in slot order win.

- **Gun.** Hold its trigger when distance ≤96% of live range, `ammo` >0, `reload` ≤0, and both slew-adjusted errors are strictly below their basic tolerances. There is no controller cooldown gate or extra LOS test to the led point. Request early `reload` if it is not already reloading and its ammo is below `mag`, there is no shot target, farm drone or farm crate, and either ammo is ≤60% of `mag` or the last threat was >3 s ago. An obscured mech alone does not prevent this reload. The server also reloads an empty magazine.
- **Lightning.** On any shootable target, hold the trigger if distance is < live range minus 1.5, the instant yaw error is < half the live cone in radians minus 0.05, and energy is ≥ the live per-shot cost. No pitch or cooldown test. The trigger can be held along with the gun or laser, using the original measured torso aim for each test.
- **Laser with a shot target.** Only against a mech within 97% of its live range. Let full charge be the live required charge time. Below full minus 0.051 s, hold the trigger and keep the default aim until current charge is strictly >45% of full, then command instant aim. From full minus 0.051 s onward, command instant aim and release when raw yaw error is <90% of the basic yaw tolerance and raw pitch error is < the basic pitch tolerance. Otherwise keep holding. These checks have no explicit energy, cooldown or charge-hold-time gate in this branch. A missing or out-of-range qualifying target leaves the trigger off unless the precharge rule applies, so an existing charge can release as a result.
- **Early lethal release, squad only.** Before the ordinary laser hold, from `min_charge` inclusive up to full minus 0.051 s exclusive, interpolate damage linearly from `min_frac` of live damage to full damage by current charge divided by live full charge. Multiply by 1 plus `config.cubes.damage_per_cube` times the lesser of own cube count and `config.cubes.stat_cap`. Against a mortar carrier, multiply again by 1 plus live `cookoff`. If 80% of this estimated damage is ≥ target HP and the same raw alignment checks pass, command instant aim and release immediately. The estimate uses those factors alone, with no separate armor or rear-hit adjustment.
- **Precharge.** With no shot-target mech and an accepted obscured fallback, hold the laser if its cooldown is ≤0.05 s, foe distance is strictly < live laser range, and observed charge is < full charge plus `hold_s` minus 0.35 s. This happens after regular weapon decisions, even during a movement override or with a farm shot. Precharge does not supply its own aim. At or beyond that threshold the trigger defaults off in this case. The kit defaults for the charge calculation are full 0.75 s, minimum 0.15 s, minimum fraction 0.35, extra hold 2 s, damage 315, cook-off 0.6 and range 280 m. The server governs forced release, shorting, charge slowdown and the actual cooldown.
- **Mortar if already equipped.** The pickup policy keeps it out of a normal loadout. Its firing branch takes a shot-target mech at 16–200 m inclusive and cooldown ≤0.25 s. Start the tube at predicted muzzle XY and feet Z plus own height plus 0.5. Aim toward target aim-point Z minus half target height. Use live shell speed and gravity and reject either ≤0. Iterate 3 times from flight time 0, leading target XY by 0.075 s plus flight time and using the lower ballistic arc for that flat distance and height difference. Reject a negative discriminant or flat distance <1. Update flight time by dividing flat distance by the product of speed and cosine of pitch. Reject pitch beyond live limits, or a solid point along the arc sampled with a clock starting at 0.1 s and advancing by 0.1 s while strictly below 90% of flight time. Use the mortar aim if the first gun is absent, reloading, at exactly zero ammo or outside 96% of live range. Trigger for slew-adjusted yaw <0.04 and pitch <0.03.
- **Aim when there is no shootable target.** In order, face the nearest awareness mech that is engaged or hit us <6 s ago, the recorded drone bearing if the drone hit was at time >0 and <2.5 s ago, the last incoming bearing if <2.5 s old, or the movement bearing plus 1.15 times the sine of 1.3 times match time. If stationary and none applies, add 0.35 to actual yaw. Use pitch 0. These choices use the final movement direction after dodging.

## 5. Movement, cover and obstacles

### Precedence each observation

Start with zero movement and boost off. Use the first applicable movement rule below. An upgrade pick is made before this list. Aiming, trigger, reload and local pickup rules run after it and can coexist with any selected movement.

1. **Escape a disconnected area.** Follow the launch and landing logic below. This also wins when it calls for waiting in place.
2. **Continue an unstuck move.** Use the stored random heading while its 0.5 s deadline is still in the future.
3. **Avoid a predicted mortar impact.** Take the first valid shell-evasion heading below. Boost at energy >15.
4. **Urgent zone travel.** If required by §7 and zone goal distance >2, route toward it. Boost at energy >5 if outside or if time plus travel estimate reaches the deadline without the 3 s urgency allowance.
5. **Retreat.** If the retreat flag is set, use §6 unless the finish rule bypasses retreat or it is late match. Successful cover travel boosts above 10 energy when moving.
6. **Hide along the last damage bearing, opening only.** With no shot-target mech, a last incoming hit <2.5 s ago and no late match, prepend a synthetic mech 80 m away along the saved bearing at our feet Z to the cover threat list. If cover search succeeds, use it and boost above 30 energy when moving. An obscured fallback does not block this branch.
7. **Fight.** Use the shot target or accepted obscured fallback. The following readiness and finishing rules feed the fight sampler. Boost if holding lightning, foe distance >50, energy >55 and the sightline preference is positive. In squad, the close cube override in §7 can replace this move while retaining any chosen boost and the foe for aiming.
8. **Advisory zone travel.** If advised by §7 and the goal is >2 away, travel without a baseline boost.
9. **Ordinary loot or fallback.** Follow the winning loot goal (§7). With no eligible item, travel to the zone goal when it is >3 away and either we are in squad or the next radius is ≤30. Otherwise opening seeks a home point (§8) and squad idles. Opening follows the home point only when it is >3 away. With no danger and last incoming hit >6 s ago, scale home movement by the lesser of 1 and 4.6 divided by live walk speed.

### Range keeping and fight-step score

- Use sightline preference +1 if a held gun has ammo >0, reload ≤0.25 s and the foe is at most gun range minus 10, or a held laser is within 0.3 s of full (charge ≥ full minus 0.3, distance ≤ range minus 5), or a held lightning weapon has distance ≤ its range plus 6 and energy ≥3 times per-shot cost. Otherwise use −1. In squad, simply holding laser or lightning forces +1 regardless of readiness. In opening, if our laser does not qualify as nearly charged and in range, any fresh charging mech whose yaw points within <0.3 of us forces −1.6. Thus a gun-only reloader often seeks a blocked ray, while a squad laser carrier seeks a clear one. This cover behavior uses the fight sampler, separately from the retreat cover search. Cornered retreat and drone farming pass +1 directly.
- Start strafe side at +1. In each fight-sampler call, when time has reached the strafe deadline, draw once and flip if the draw is <0.6. Draw again and schedule the next check 0.3 s plus 0.7 times the draw ahead. Strafe direction is the vector toward the foe rotated 90° counterclockwise for +1 and clockwise for −1. Normalize the toward vector using horizontal distance plus 10⁻⁹. If any rejected 6 m candidate heading has strafe dot product >0.9, flip once after choosing this tick’s move and set the next check to time plus 0.5 s.
- Try standing still first, then 16 headings starting east and rotating counterclockwise by π/8 at 6 m. Opening also tries those 16 at 12 m. Total 33 opening and 17 squad candidates before filtering. Discard moving candidates unless the destination cell is free and the full segment is walkable. Return a unit input in the winning heading, or zero for staying. The candidate step length is a look-ahead distance.
- For each candidate start at −1.1 times the absolute error between resulting foe distance and preferred range, minus 0.4 times candidate step length. If the ray from candidate walking surface plus own muzzle height to the foe’s midpoint is clear, add 45 times the sightline preference. A blocked ray contributes 0. In opening, a moving 6 m candidate whose ray matches the desired clear/blocked state gains 8 if any of 8 additional probes reaches a free cell with the opposite ray state. Probe 7 m from the candidate, starting east and rotating by π/4. That extra probe requires a free cell only.
- Subtract 30 for every other mech in the ≤2.5 s awareness list within 170 m inclusive of the candidate whose eye has a clear ray to the candidate walking surface plus 60% of our height. Use recorded positions even for stale awareness entries. Subtract the zone penalty in §7. For the closest remembered cube strictly within 40 m and <4 m vertically of our current position, add 1.5 times the decrease in distance to that cube. If obstacle clearance is <4 m, subtract 2.5 times its shortfall from 4.
- With positive sightline preference, add 9 times the candidate heading’s dot product with the strafe direction. Staying scores −8 and gets +14 when the sightline preference is negative and its ray is blocked. Maximize total score and keep the first candidate on an exact tie. Candidate height comes from the map’s walking surface even for staying or airborne decisions.

### Dodges, boost and jump

- **Mortar.** For each observed shell in order, march its observed position up to 4 s in 0.05 s steps. Advance position first, then subtract gravity times 0.05 from vertical velocity. Use configured mortar gravity, or 22 if it is ≤0. Stop at the first solid point. Try to evade if the impact is inside configured splash radius plus our radius plus 2, and the accumulated loop clock (starting at 0, before each 0.05 s increment) is <2.5 s. Use the direction away from the impact, or the shell’s horizontal velocity rotated 90° counterclockwise if impact distance <0.5. Try offsets of 0, +0.7, −0.7, +1.4, −1.4 in that order. The first with a walkable 6 m ray wins. If none works, check the next shell.
- **Bullet and aimed-laser overlay.** Apply after every primary move except escape and active unstuck. For each non-mortar projectile with squared 3D speed ≥1, project time to closest approach to own body at 56% height using its 3D velocity and our current position. Keep times in (0, 0.7] and closest horizontal offset strictly inside own radius plus 1.1, with horizontal projectile speed ≥1. Accumulate a unit vector perpendicular to projectile XY velocity, turned toward the side away from the projected offset (start counterclockwise, reverse on positive dot product). In raw observation order, for each charging enemy mech within 0.15 radians inclusive of facing us, add a perpendicular of magnitude 1.5 using the bearing from the enemy to us rotated by the current strafe side times π/2. There is no range, pitch or shot visibility test on these charging threats.
- If the accumulated dodge vector has norm >0.01, normalize it, blend 0.4 of the primary move with 0.9 of the dodge, and cap length at 1. This blend does not recheck obstacles or the zone. Within the nonzero-dodge branch, if any charging mech contributed and energy >25, set boost. Otherwise preserve any boost chosen earlier. Send boost only with nonzero movement. The server controls whether boost runs or jets.
- **Stuck jump.** Outside escape, nonzero commanded movement with measured horizontal speed <1.2 m/s while grounded starts or continues a timer. Once it has run >0.7 s, request jump immediately, choose a uniform random heading, set its 0.5 s continuation for subsequent ticks, discard the cached path and reset the stuck timer to −1. The triggering tick keeps its already selected movement. Any idle, ungrounded, faster or escape tick resets the timer. This jump has no energy check.

### World tests and routing

- Load `v2/world.json` and `v2/terrain.i16` from `hello.map.dir`, default `/arena`. Treat terrain as a 1 m grid of little-endian signed 16-bit centimetres. Multiply centimetres by binary32 0.01, store the binary32 products, and bilinearly sample after clamping grid coordinates to [0, last index minus 10⁻⁶]. Use even-odd containment over the outer prism ring and its holes. Point solidity means below ground, or inside a prism footprint and strictly between its base and top. Read at least 2 bytes per sample and ignore the declared terrain step. If loading fails, use zero terrain with 1070 × 724 samples from (−513, −334), bounds X [−512, 555] and Y [−333, 388], and no prisms.
- A clear shot ray tests prisms and terrain, ignoring trees, canopy and other units. A ray starting inside a prism, including its top and bottom heights, is blocked. Otherwise test the segment against vertical edges and entering top or bottom faces at ray parameter [0, 1), accepting edge endpoints. Ignore side intersections when the XY cross product with the edge is below 10⁻⁹ in magnitude, and face intersections when absolute Z displacement is ≤10⁻⁹. Before exact prism tests, intersect the segment with the three axis-aligned prism slabs and treat a displacement with magnitude <10⁻⁹ as stationary. Use 16 m prism buckets with origin 16 m below each map minimum and size equal to the ceiling of map span divided by 16 plus 3 per axis. Walk toward the endpoint cell for up to 4,096 cells, ignoring axis motion with magnitude ≤10⁻⁹. On an exact crossing tie step along Y before X.
- For terrain, test below-ground at the start, then advance by 0.5 m of horizontal travel per sample up to the endpoint. For horizontal length ≤10⁻⁹ use only the start and endpoint. If a sample is below ground, the ray is blocked. To skip samples, partition the terrain grid into 8 × 8 blocks. Each block’s maximum includes the three adjoining blocks to its east, north and northeast where present. At each ray position in a real block, find the next forward boundary. Add 10⁻⁶ in ray parameter even when the boundary equals the current position, and clamp to the endpoint. If both ends exceed the block maximum, advance to that boundary and restart there. Otherwise advance to the next 0.5 m sample or endpoint and test that point. There is a cap of 100,001 iterations before reporting no terrain hit. The bot bisects a detected crossing 20 times, which does not affect the clear/blocked result.
- For walking-surface height, start at terrain. Up to 8 passes in map order, raise the surface to a containing prism’s higher top if its base is at most 1.5 above the current surface, or if it is a bridge with its top <6.5 above the original terrain. The escape height test uses the same stack without the bridge exception. For the 1 m obstacle raster used on high ground and for home exposure, scan-fill prism ring intersections at row Y plus 0.0001, starting at the ceiling of the prism’s minimum Y grid index. Use inclusive integer spans from ceiling of the paired left edge to floor of the right. Sort vertical intervals by base, merging upward when base ≤ current top plus 1.5, skipping intervals whose tops are already below or at that surface. Store separate higher slab endpoints in binary32. A cell is an obstacle when its merged top is >1.2 above terrain, or a separated slab begins <10.5 above the surface at that step and is not a bridge with top <6.5 above terrain. Raster queries round to the nearest coordinate and clamp to the grid.
- Build a 2 m navigation grid from the terrain origin with dimensions (terrain sample count plus 1) divided by 2, rounded down. Quantize to the nearest grid point, clamped to the grid. At cell centre use the walking surface. Block roofs >1.5 above terrain when the last prism to raise the surface is a non-bridge and any point that cannot fit a body of radius 2.6 and height 9. Fitting requires a 2.6 m margin against map bounds and no prism overlapping the vertical band from surface plus 1.2 to surface plus 9, with its polygon containing the center or an edge at distance <2.6. Keep the surface as a 32-bit float for grid rise checks. Clearance is four-neighbour distance in 2 m increments from the nearest blocked cell, capped at 20.
- Grid steps require a free destination, binary32 rise ≤ binary32 2.2 orthogonally or ≤ binary32 3.1 diagonally, and for diagonals both adjacent orthogonal cells free. Downhill steps have no drop limit. Use east, west, north, south, northeast, southeast, northwest, southwest order. Define the main component as the largest four-neighbour component with both directions passable, keeping the first discovered in row-major order on a size tie. A blocked cell snaps to a free cell in the first square ring containing one, up to 20 rings. Minimize squared grid distance within that ring and keep the first in Y-then-X increasing order on ties.
- A walkable straight segment checks equally spaced samples, stopping at the end, with count the floor of length in metres plus 1. Skip samples in the previous cell. Reject a new cell if blocked or its binary32 height minus the previous height, rounded to binary32, exceeds binary32 3.1. The starting cell is accepted without a blocked-cell test. For travel, stop within <0.8 of goal. If feet are >3 above terrain at a raster obstacle, head directly toward the goal. Otherwise head straight there only if <45 and the straight walk test passes, or if routing falls back to direct travel.
- Cache a route until absent, >2.5 s old or its goal shifts >5 m. Snap start and goal to free cells. Use weighted A* for at most 22,000 priority-queue pops, including duplicate pops. Orthogonal edge cost is 2, diagonal is 2 times 1.4142, each multiplied by 1 plus 0.15 times clearance shortfall below 4 at the destination. The heuristic is 1.15 times 2 times the larger grid-axis difference plus 0.4142 times the smaller, with the two axis terms summed before multiplication. Round each edge operation, clearance multiplier, running cost and heap key in binary32. Evaluate the heuristic as the axis sum, then times 2, then times 1.15, then convert to binary32. Queue by the sum of path cost and heuristic, update only for a cheaper path and do not reopen closed cells. On failure use the path to the popped cell with the smallest heuristic, retaining the first on ties. With no path, use direct travel. The binary heap leaves an equal key below its parent on insertion and sifts after removal using strict comparisons, checking the left child first.
- For path following, among the current waypoint and the next 9, choose the nearest, first on ties. Discard the path and head directly if that waypoint is >12 away. From up to 14 indices beyond it, scan backwards for the farthest index with a walkable straight segment. If it is the last waypoint, within <4 of the exact goal, and the direct goal ray is walkable, travel directly to the goal. If no shortcut exists, head toward the next waypoint, or the goal when already at the end. Any direct unit heading becomes zero for a displacement <10⁻⁶.

### Disconnected-area escape

- While grounded, clear the escape state if already in the main component (using the nearest free cell), or if >3 m above terrain at a raster obstacle. Otherwise find a launch-land pair, recomputing if invalid or >6 s old. Search reachable cells by the eight-neighbour step rules with a breadth-first queue of up to 60,000 visits. Find each cell’s nearest main-component cell by a separate multi-source eight-neighbour flood from the main component in grid order, extending up to 20 steps through all cell types. Minimize 2 times walking step count plus 4 times launch-to-landing distance, retaining the first on ties. Extend the landing point by 5 m along the launch-to-land direction if their separation is >0.1 and the extended point lies strictly within bounds in a free main-component cell.
- If there is a plan, walk to within 2.5 of launch. Wait there until energy is ≥ the smaller of 70 and 70% of max energy, then request jump and apply landing steering and boost immediately. In flight continue only with an escape jump in progress. Stop horizontal movement within <1.5 of the landing point. Otherwise head toward it and boost when feet Z is below 2.5 above the maximum non-deck surface height along the heading, sampled from 0 through the smaller of 30 m and remaining distance in 1 m increments, and energy is >1. A grounded observation reevaluates the escape conditions.

## 6. Finishing, retreat and recovery

- **Finish instead of retreating.** A shot target at distance <120 with HP <200 or HP <half our raw HP bypasses the retreat movement branch. The HP test is inside the distance condition. This applies in both phases, leaves the retreat flag intact, and has lower movement priority than escape, unsticking, shell evasion and urgent zone travel. Late match also skips retreat movement with the flag intact.
- **Close in to finish, squad only.** When in the fight branch, if the movement foe is below 38% of its max HP, our HP is >42%, and crowd count ≤2, set all three mech preferred distances to 14 m for this sampler call. The 64/80 m floors still apply. This uses the movement foe, including an obscured one, and has no separate 120 m requirement or dedicated boost. Failing any gate restores normal range, and a squad cube detour can still replace the movement.
- **Opening retreat flag.** If the flag is unset, set it when our HP is <38% and last threat <2 s ago, or when swamped. Swamped means crowd ≥3, crowd exactly 2 with our HP <65%, or a shot-target laser carrier >55 m away while we lack laser and our HP is <80%. Entering by this rule resets the cover search time to −1. Clear the flag only if no longer swamped and either our HP is >80% or it is >55% with last threat >7 s ago. A recent incoming hit can keep that last-threat timer fresh for nearly 3 s after the hit.
- **Squad retreat flag.** The opening calculation still executes first, including any cover-clock reset. Then override the flag. Count all fresh mechs at distance <160, whether or not engaged or behind cover. Refresh the losing timestamp if there are at least 2 and our HP is <55%, or at least 3 and our HP is <80%. Also refresh it on a shot-target duel if our HP fraction is <35% and theirs is > our share plus 0.10, using its `hp` divided by the greater of 1 and its `max_hp`. With a shot target below 25% HP, our HP >20% and at most 2 nearby, suppress the refresh even if a losing condition held. The flag lasts while the losing timestamp is >0 and its age <1.5 s. Suppressing the refresh leaves a running timer intact. The separate finish movement bypass can take effect immediately.
- **Retreat destination.** Try cover first. If no cover and a shot target exists, use the combat sampler with sightline preference +1. Otherwise travel toward the zone goal. If that gives zero movement and a drone hit at time >0 is <3 s old, move perpendicular to its recorded bearing, rotating +π/2 when match time modulo 3 is ≤1.5 and −π/2 otherwise.
- **Cover search.** Use threats strictly within 230 m based on their recorded positions. With more than 4, sort by increasing distance without a tie key and keep the first 4. With no threats, fail immediately. A point is covered if none of their eyes can trace a clear ray to our walking-surface position at full height or half height. Reuse a successful cover goal for age <0.4 s. On recompute, stay if already covered and the zone penalty is 0. Otherwise breadth-first search from the snapped free cell with the eight-neighbour step rules. Check only cells whose grid indices sum to an even number and that are at least 1 step away. Check at most 350 such cells, stop beyond 50 steps or if 2 times step count exceeds the best score plus 30. Minimize 2 times step count plus zone penalty minus 0.25 times the nearer of 80 m and the nearest threat distance. Keep the first on ties. Travel to the result and stop within <1.5 m. If there is no result, the caller’s fallback applies.
- **Repair.** Recovery is the retreat cancellation above and the server’s passive in-zone heal after time without taking or dealing damage (`regen_delay`, 5 s by kit default, then `regen_per_s`, 45 HP/s before upgrades). Storm damage prevents that recovery. Retreat allows shots at mechs and defensive shots at drones. Retreat or own HP <50% suppresses ordinary farming shots. A selected farm drone or crate can still block an early reload even if its fire is suppressed.

## 7. Zone, cubes, crates, upgrades and drones

### Zone

- Keep the reported current circle whenever `shrink_start` changes or time is ≤ `shrink_start`. To estimate it at another time, keep that hold circle through shrink start, and also if end ≤ start. Otherwise interpolate all three values (center XY and radius) linearly toward the next circle, clamping the fraction to [0, 1]. At the current time, use the reported circle before or at shrink start. Afterward use it if its radius or X center differs from the held value by >0.01. Otherwise use the interpolation. A Y-only report change does not trigger that override.
- For a location and pad, set deadline to infinity if it is within next radius minus pad. Otherwise sample 25 equally spaced times from shrink start to shrink end inclusive and return the first at which the point exceeds the sampled radius minus pad. If none does, use shrink end. For our movement deadline use pad 8.
- Travel goal is the closest point in a smaller circle around the next center, whose radius is the greater of 0 and next radius minus the margin minus 5. Margin is 30% of next radius clamped to [3, 30]. If already in that inner circle or distance to center <1, use the present spot. If next radius is ≤30, use the next center directly. Travel estimate is 1.45 times distance to this goal divided by live walk speed, plus 4 s. Urgent if `in_zone` is false or current time plus estimate plus 3 s reaches the deadline. Advisory if time plus estimate plus 14 s reaches it.
- Zone penalty at a candidate point uses signed edge distance (distance to center minus radius). If current signed distance is >−5, add 150 plus 5 times the distance above −5. If next signed distance is >−5, also add 25 plus 0.8 times that next distance above −5, scaled by 1 minus (time until shrink start divided by 25), clamped to [0.1, 1]. This is additive across both circles. Damage per second is not part of the controller’s travel or penalty math.
